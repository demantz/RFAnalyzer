    Troubleshooting & FAQs

    SDR Device Not Recognized

    If you get errors like "Source not available" or "Error with Source", the app is not able to communicate with the SDR device.

    Possible reasons are:

    • Wrong SDR selected in Source Tab.
    • Phone’s USB port doesn’t provide enough power.
    • USB OTG adapter is faulty.
    • Another app is already using the SDR.

    Solution:

    1. Try a powered USB hub.
    2. Ensure no other apps are using the SDR.
    3. Try different USB OTG adapters.
    4. Turn on logging in the Settings and look in the log file for more details.

    USB permission denied

    Android asks once for permission to use the SDR. If you denied it, tap Start again and allow access.

    Network Sources (rtl_tcp, KiwiSDR)

    • Android 17 and newer: a server in your own network (rtl_tcp, a local KiwiSDR) needs RF Analyzer's local network permission. If Android no longer asks, allow it in the app's settings under Permissions → Nearby devices.
    • Short network stalls (up to 15 seconds) are bridged; after that the source stops with "Streaming stopped unexpectedly".
    • A KiwiSDR with no free channel slots is busy: pick another receiver.

    Purchase Not Recognized

    Purchases belong to the Google account that is active in the Play Store. Switch the Play Store to the account you bought with, return to RF Analyzer and tap Check again in the About tab.

    SDR Stops During Use (USB Connection Lost)

    A loose USB plug or cable makes the SDR disconnect for a moment; the app then shows "... stopped streaming unexpectedly (USB connection lost?)". When the same SDR is connected again within 10 seconds, the analyzer restarts by itself - also with the app in the background, which stays there. Meanwhile the notification says "Waiting for ... to be reconnected"; its Stop button ends the wait.

    When the SDR comes back, Android asks whether to open RF Analyzer for it; tick Always open RF Analyzer when ... is connected so that later reconnects work without the question. (Outside of these 10 seconds, and unless Start When an SDR Is Plugged In is enabled, Android doesn't offer RF Analyzer when an SDR is plugged in.)

    Performance Issues (Slow FFT, Choppy Audio)

    Older hardware might not be powerful enough to ensure smooth operation of the app. In this case try the following:

    • Reduce the sample rate of the source.
    • Lower the Max Frame Rate in the Display tab.
    • Keep the Waterfall Speed at 30 fps or below: above that, the FFT frames overlap and cost more processing.
    • With Manual FFT Size on, choose a smaller FFT Size (otherwise it is chosen automatically).

    If the app shows an Overload warning, see Processing Overload.

    Processing Overload

    The app processes the signal of the SDR in real time: every second of signal has to be filtered, demodulated and decoded within one second. If your device is too slow for the current settings, it can't keep up and has to throw parts of the signal away. When this happens, the Demodulation tab shows the warning chip Overload – samples dropped; tap its ? to open this page. The audio stutters or is choppy. The chip disappears a few seconds after the overload stops.

    What you can do (start at the top, it usually helps most):

    1. Reduce the sample rate of the source in the Source tab. This lowers the load of the whole processing chain. You see less bandwidth in the FFT, but the demodulator only needs the part around the channel anyway.
    2. Lower the Max Frame Rate and the Waterfall Speed in the Display tab; with Manual FFT Size on, also the FFT Size. Drawing the waterfall competes with the demodulator for processing power.
    3. Enable Low Performance Mode and lower the filter quality until the warning goes away.
    4. Close other apps running in the background and turn off battery saver mode, which slows down the processor.

    Details for the curious: enable Show Debug Information to see the load of the busiest processing stage in the top right corner of the FFT (green below 70%, yellow up to 100%, red above), and, during an overload, how much of the signal was dropped and where.

    Poor Signal Quality / High Noise

    • Use a good antenna & place it in an open area.
    • Disable automatic gain and manually adjust gain.
    • Increase sample rate to reduce aliasing (HackRF).

    App Crashes

    • If possible, submit crash reports via Android.
    • RF Analyzer can log system errors; check the Settings.
